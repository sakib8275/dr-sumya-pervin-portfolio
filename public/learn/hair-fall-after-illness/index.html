Hair fall after illness or childbirth — and when it is something else

Heavy shedding a few months after a fever, surgery, crash diet or childbirth is common, and it usually settles. But hair loss has several types, and each is treated differently.

Written and reviewed by Dr. Sumya Pervin, FCPS (Skin & VD), BMDC A-59492 · Last reviewed October 2026.

Book a consultation See prices

The commonest type

Telogen effluvium: shedding after illness, stress, dieting or childbirth. It usually recovers as the trigger passes, and it is worth treating the cause rather than the shed.

Other types

Pattern hair loss (in men and women), patchy loss (alopecia areata), and scalp disease such as dandruff or fungal infection all look different and need different treatment.

How the type is found

Trichoscopy (a magnified look at the scalp), a pull test, and blood tests only when they change treatment — for example iron, thyroid or vitamin D.

What helps

  • Minoxidil topical, or low-dose tablets for suitable patients
  • Finasteride for suitable men
  • PRP therapy as an add-on for pattern hair loss
  • Steroid injections for alopecia areata
  • Treating the trigger such as low iron, thyroid problems or scalp inflammation

What does not work

  • Hair oils marketed as “regrowth” treatments
  • Supplements without a proven deficiency
  • PRP for shedding that will settle on its own

When to see a doctor quickly

Rapid patchy loss, a painful or scarring scalp, or hair loss with other symptoms such as weight change.
