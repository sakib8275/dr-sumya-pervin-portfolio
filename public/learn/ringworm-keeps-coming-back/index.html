Why ringworm keeps coming back — and the cream that makes it worse

Fungal skin infections (tinea, ringworm) have become harder to treat across South Asia. A common reason is a cream that contains a steroid: it calms the itch for a few days while the infection spreads underneath.

Written and reviewed by Dr. Sumya Pervin, FCPS (Skin & VD), BMDC A-59492 · Last reviewed October 2026.

Book a consultation See prices

What it looks like

Itchy, ring-shaped or spreading patches in the groin, waist, under the breasts, buttocks, or on the body, feet and nails. It spreads within families through shared towels and clothes.

The cream that makes it worse

Steroid or “mixed” creams hide the infection instead of clearing it. If a cream seems to work for a few days and then the rash returns worse, suspect a steroid. Antifungal treatment needs to continue for weeks, not days.

How it is confirmed

A scraping looked at under our microscope (a KOH test), with a result in the same visit, from the licence stage onwards. Sometimes a culture is sent to a partner lab.

How it is treated

  • Antifungal creams for limited infection, used two weeks beyond clearing
  • Antifungal tablets for widespread or recurrent infection, at the right dose and duration
  • Household plan: treat close contacts, wash laundry at high temperature, use separate towels
  • Nails need months of treatment; we explain realistic outcomes

What will not work

  • Any cream with a steroid in it
  • Stopping when the itch stops
  • Treating yourself but not your family

When to see a doctor quickly

Painful, pus-filled patches on the scalp (especially in children), or infection in people with diabetes or low immunity.
